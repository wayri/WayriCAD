# QuickTherm coupled-board transient screen

`wayricad-therm-transient MODEL.json --output result.json --csv trace.csv --svg temperatures.svg` runs a read-only, lumped thermal RC calculation for exactly four boards in the declared airflow topology. The command checks each saved `.kicad_pcb` with KiCad, verifies that each source reference exists, hashes the source bytes before and after solving, and writes the full input model, source hashes, time series, checks and assumptions to JSON. CSV records time in seconds, temperatures in °C, and power/heat flow in W; the SVG plots junction and downstream-air temperatures. The CLI prints SHA-256 hashes of its output files. `--require-checks-pass` returns a nonzero status on FAIL or UNKNOWN. This command is independent of the native steady-state viewport.

Use a reviewed JSON object with an explicit `air_topology`. `serial_boards` takes four boards in downstream order. `parallel_passages` takes four side-by-side boards, `air_flow_direction: "-X"`, `blade_pitch_mm`, and four positive `passage_flow_fractions` summing to one. It does not heat Board 2 with Board 1's exhaust. The latter path orders each board's component stations by saved footprint X, highest to lowest, and reports local air temperature and heat transfer at each station plus the mixed exhaust. The board's entered `hA` is divided equally among those stations; this is a lumped screen, not a resolved surface/velocity field. The native saved-board wrapper binds X positions to KiCad footprints and rejects a supplied X more than 0.5 mm away.

Each board needs a unique `name`, `board_path`, `capacity_j_k`, `air_conductance_curve` and at least one `components` entry. A component needs `reference`, `capacity_j_k`, `r_junction_board_k_w` and its actual `fixed_power_w` or an explicit `resistive_loss`. It can have a `package_body` with its own heat capacity, junction-to-body thermal resistance and body-to-air conductance curve. It can also have `isolated_sink` with measured/assumed sink capacity, junction-to-sink resistance, an airflow conductance curve, and nonempty `isolation_evidence`. These are explicit lumped package/contact nodes; a plastic body outline alone supplies none of their values. Optional `maximum_junction_c` and global `max_local_air_c` produce conditional PASS/FAIL checks. Missing limits yield UNKNOWN. The solver never substitutes a data-sheet maximum power rating for actual dissipation.

An `isolated_sink` requires `contact_kind`. Use `generic_isolated` for an ordinary electrically isolated attachment. Use `metal_drain` for a live-drain path, with `drain_net`, `working_voltage_v` and `insulation_rating_v` in addition to the thermal inputs. The software rejects an entered insulation rating below the declared working voltage. This is an input-consistency check, not verification of dielectric construction, spacing, hi-pot testing or assembly safety.

An optional `fixed_boundary` on a board declares `temperature_c`, finite `contact_r_k_w`, and nonempty `contact_evidence` for a maintained fixture/mount temperature. It contributes explicit signed `fixture_heat_w` to the energy balance. A mounting hole or ground plane alone does not establish this contact.

Supply `inlet_air_c`, `duration_s`, `step_s`, `air_density_kg_m3`, `air_cp_j_kgk`, `fan_curve` as increasing single-fan `[flow_m3_s, pressure_pa]` points, `fans_parallel`, `system_k_pa_s2_m6`, and `bypass_fraction`. The fan curve must cover the intersection with `Δp = K Q²`; the parallel fan group evaluates each fan at total flow divided by the number of fans. `serial_boards` uses `Q_board = Q_fan(1 − bypass)` for every serial stage. `parallel_passages` divides that nonbypass total according to the explicit passage fractions. `blade_pitch_mm` is retained as an assembly input but does not magically determine the fractions or pressure coefficient. Every `air_conductance_curve` is increasing `[board-channel flow_m3_s, hA_w_k]` points that bracket that passage's operating flow. These curves represent assembled board/sink heat transfer, not free-air CFM. A fan model number alone is insufficient; do not infer installed flow from its free-air rating.

At each time step, implicit Euler solves each board's board, junction, optional package body and optional sink nodes. Serial boards pass calculated outlet air to the next board: `T_out = T_in + Q_air/(ρ Q_board c_p)`. In a parallel passage, each streamwise station exchanges heat with its local air using a lumped `hA` and conserves `Q_air = ρ Q_passage c_p (T_out−T_in)`. The optional ohmic law is `P(T) = I² R_ref[1 + α(T − T_ref)]`; it is iterated with the thermal and air solution and rejects negative resistance. It does not represent a MOSFET linear-mode `I·V_DS` law or qualify DC SOA. Energy residual is `P − Q_air − Q_fixture − Σ C ΔT/Δt`; the solver rejects a numerical imbalance above its tolerance.

`status: computed_unqualified` means the numerical model completed, even when a conditional check passes. It does not mean that contacts, dielectric isolation, fan flow, thermal capacities, local air recirculation or component SOA are verified. The model has no spatial copper, via, radiation, package hotspot or airflow-field resolution; use the separate layer-resolved steady model and physical test evidence for those questions. Its downstream-air calculation treats air advection as quasi-steady within each thermal time step, without duct transport delay. Validate the entered network against temperature measurements and time-step refinement before treating a check as engineering evidence.

For a repeated-board demonstration, `examples/thermal-demo-transient.json` uses four named copies of the public demonstration PCB. Its powers, capacities, fan curve and heat-transfer curves are hypothetical and must be replaced for real hardware. Run from the repository root so its relative board paths resolve correctly. Keep private board paths and measurement files out of public reports.

### Flow sensitivity when the fan curve is incomplete

If the complete fan pressure–flow curve or assembled system resistance is unavailable, supply `prescribed_total_flow_m3_s` in place of both `fan_curve` and `system_k_pa_s2_m6`. The output marks the operating point `prescribed_sensitivity_input` and leaves pressure unknown. This is a conditional flow sweep, not a claim that the fan delivers that flow in the assembly. Run several plausible flows and obtain installed flow data or validated fan/system curves before selecting an operating point. `bypass_fraction` and, for parallel passages, the entered flow fractions still apply.

The `parallel_passages` mode uses four effective per-board channels. Those are a compact model of the blade passages and exterior bypass, not a literal count of physical gaps. The local air temperature is a streamwise lumped station estimate, not a velocity or boundary-layer field. Package-body, pad-contact, airflow conductance and isolated-sink inputs need reviewed values; missing values cannot be inferred from a footprint outline or fan nameplate.

`examples/thermal-demo-parallel-flow.json` illustrates a hypothetical 20 CFM total-flow sensitivity case using four copies of the public demonstration board. Its equal flow split, `hA`, powers and capacities are teaching inputs, not measurements. Run it from the repository root with `wayricad-therm-transient quick_therm_plugin/examples/thermal-demo-parallel-flow.json --output result.json` and inspect `fan_operating_point.source`, `air_stages` and check details in the JSON.

### Steady copper-field mesh checks

The separate steady multilayer solver accepts `source_contact_pad_numbers` in `thermal_network_settings`, mapping a dissipating reference to a saved pad number. It uses the pad's actual copper polygon for heat injection and excludes overlapping drilled holes, including holes belonging to separate vias. Without this mapping, it uses the footprint bounding-box proxy. The default `copper_raster_mode` is `area_face`, which clips saved copper polygons to cells and face bands rather than sampling three points; `barrel_contact_mode` defaults to an annulus stencil outside the drill. These remain reduced-order contact and axial-barrel approximations.

Mesh acceptance compares the two finest grids' component temperatures, source peaks, layer peaks and sampled spatial fields. `minimum_source_cells` uses contact area divided by cell area, so a tiny source overlapping four cells does not count as four resolved cells. Add `phase_offset_fraction: [0.5, 0.5]` to repeat the finest grid with shifted interior edges and expose grid-phase sensitivity. A reported peak whose cell center falls in a drilled void fails acceptance. `source_peak_c` and `junction_peak_proxy_c` are sampled indicators, not qualified die maxima. Energy closure and a passing grid check do not validate copper, package, airflow or fixture material data.

Source heat fractions are checked against the declared total component power before solving. If no overlapped contact cell has a center outside a drilled hole, the solver conserves that power in nearby non-drilled nodes and labels the source `saved_pad_copper_polygon_unresolved_annulus_proxy`; mesh acceptance fails for that source. Likewise, an unresolved barrel annulus uses a nearest-outside-drill stencil for a diagnostic solve and fails mesh acceptance. These fallbacks keep energy bookkeeping intact but are not evidence that the pad or via contact is spatially resolved.
